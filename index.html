A question India's mining belt keeps asking

"Why am I suffering?"

Families living beside India's copper, iron and chromium mines have been asking it for decades. Sanjeevani starts with their answer, not ours.

Two ways this land can go

Left alone, or engineered to heal.

Remediation and recovery are usually treated as two separate problems — clean the site, or extract what's left in it. Sanjeevani is built to do both with the same organism.

Left as is

Tailings stay exposed for decades, leaching metal into soil and groundwater — unusable for anything.

With Sanjeevani

The same ground, recolonised — metal pulled out and held in biomass that can be harvested and reclaimed.

What if the organism that heals this land could also hand back what the mine was looking for?

Introducing

Sanjeevani is a bioremediation and biomining organism.

We engineer the moss Physcomitrium patens — a chassis with no cuticle and no true vascular tissue, so every cell meets the water and metal around it directly. Four genes do the work: two transporters pull copper and iron-group metals in, a chelator holds them in place, and a third transporter reaches further, toward rare earths.

03 — Dry Lab

Choosing the right parts, in silico, before touching a plate.

Two computational threads run in parallel: a docking screen to pick the best metal-binding chelator, and a genome-scale metabolic model of the moss itself.

Metallothionein docking screen

Seven candidate metallothioneins — including AtMT4a, AtMT4b, PpMT2, AtMT2a/b and AtMT3 — were cross-validated with AutoDock4 and HADDOCK against five metals: Fe, Cu, Cd, Hg and Pb.

AtMT4b ranked first, placing top-3 in all 10 of 10 metal × method combinations — no other candidate matched it.

Genome-scale metabolic model

The P. patens genome was loaded into KBase, a draft model reconstructed via PlantSEED, genes cross-checked with OrthoFinder, and viability confirmed with a preliminary flux balance analysis (FBA).

Where the model stands

468 of 1,085 total reactions — 43% — now carry gene-level evidence. Confirmed Cu/Fe/Zn transport reactions from Arabidopsis-derived models (AraGEM, AraRoot) are being validated against the P. patens network next.

Three genes do the uptake and the storage

Transporters bring metal in. A chelator keeps it there.

COPT1from Arabidopsis thaliana

High-affinity copper transporter at the plasma membrane. Cloned and confirmed — the first construct through the full pipeline. Fused in-frame to MT4b via a P2A self-cleaving peptide: pTFH15.3_COPT1::2A::MT4.

IRT1from Arabidopsis thaliana

Iron-Regulated Transporter 1 — a broad-spectrum divalent metal transporter (Fe²⁺, plus Zn, Mn, Cd) acting through the FIT/AHA2/FRO2 uptake pathway. Construct ordered: pTFH15.3_IRT1::2A::MT4.

AtMT4bmetallothionein, A. thaliana

Cysteine-rich intracellular chelator that sequesters metal once it's inside the cell — the top performer from our docking screen, chosen over six other candidates. Construct ordered alongside IRT1.

NREET1from Dicranopteris linearis

Plasma-membrane rare-earth-element transporter from a REE-hyperaccumulating fern, carried as a standalone construct to extend uptake toward lanthanum and neodymium.

“COPT1 is cloned and confirmed; IRT1 and AtMT4b constructs are ordered and moving into expression, with moss culture running in parallel.”

All constructs are built on pTFH15.3, a standard P. patens community transformation vector (~7.5 kb) carrying an Act1-driven nptII selection cassette and Pphb7 homology arms for targeted integration into the moss genome.

05 — Human Practices

Eight conversations that changed what we built.

We didn't design this project at a desk alone — it was reshaped, repeatedly, by the people closest to the problem.

Repositioning the science
  • ARCI (Dr. Raghavan Gopalan)REE recovery is midstream-limited, not primary — reframed Sanjeevani as a supplement to conventional extraction, not a replacement.
  • NALCOSimilar attempts exist globally — grounded our framing in a shared, ongoing effort rather than an unprecedented fix.
Protecting project integrity
  • Tata SteelOffered funding for a side project. We declined it to protect the core project's timeline and integrity.
Methodology changed by experts
  • Dr. Anjan Banerjee's Lab, IISER PuneShared a 1-day moss protocol and trained us directly — halving our 2-day protocol turnaround.
  • Kerala Dept. of Mining & GeologyTheir Junior Chemist flagged the limits of our assay — we switched metal-uptake quantification from AAS to ICP-MS.
The human cost, made concrete
  • IREL / Chatrapur HospitalRising kidney disease near radioactive tailings gave the project a human-health dimension, not just an ecological one.
  • OMC — Sukinda ValleyCarcinogenic chromite is restricting fungal fruiting bodies on-site — reinforcing the case for an engineered, metal-tolerant chassis.
  • Odisha State & Tribal MuseumsInterns from mining belts described displaced communities, shifting our framing from ecological to directly human.

Big is not the same as inclusive

Each pillar came from what a community actually needed — not what we assumed.

That is the difference between outreach and inclusivity.

Language access

Sessions delivered in Telugu, Odia and Marathi by speakers from matching linguistic backgrounds — not translated from English.

A real barrier, removed

Menstrual hygiene kits distributed in Odisha after learning that menstruation — not lack of interest — was what kept girls out of school.
